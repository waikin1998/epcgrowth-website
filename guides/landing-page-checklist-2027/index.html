7 Landing Page Checks Before Your 2027 Marketing Campaign

By EPC Marketing · 3 November 2026 · 3 min read

Help visitors understand your offer, trust your business and take the next step with a clear, mobile-friendly landing page.

2027 preparation guide · Written using information available on 3 November 2026. Platform features and guidance may change.

Give each campaign a clear destination

A landing page is a focused destination for a campaign. Its job is to explain one offer and help a visitor take a useful next step. It does not need to contain everything your company has ever done.

Use this checklist before your 2027 campaign launches. The recommendations are practical design choices, not a promise of a particular conversion rate.

1. Make the headline specific

Say what you offer and who it helps. A visitor arriving from an ad should recognise the same service immediately. Avoid a clever slogan that leaves the actual offer unclear.

2. Add a useful subheadline

Use a short sentence to explain the benefit, service area or next step. Leave out claims you cannot support. A specific explanation is more helpful than repeating that your business is the best.

3. Show the product or service

Choose an image that helps customers understand what they will receive. Keep important details visible on smaller screens. Optimise the file so the image does not make the page unnecessarily heavy.

4. Use genuine proof

Show approved examples, relevant company information or real testimonials. Keep the context of any results clear. Do not replace missing evidence with invented numbers or reviews.

5. Explain benefits in plain language

Connect each feature to a practical reason a customer should care. For example, an easy enquiry form can reduce the effort needed to ask a question. Short paragraphs make the page easier to scan.

6. Make the next action obvious

Choose a main action such as requesting a quote or starting a WhatsApp conversation. Explain what happens next. Check that every button goes to the intended destination on both mobile and desktop.

7. Keep forms simple and test the journey

Ask only for information needed to respond. Provide a clear confirmation after submission and test how the enquiry reaches your team. Review the information you collect and explain its use.

Before launch, ask someone unfamiliar with the business to find the offer and contact you. Note where they hesitate. Fix those points before paying to send more visitors to the page.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a landing page if I already have a website?

Not always. An existing service page can work if it matches the campaign and gives visitors a clear next step.

Can a landing page guarantee conversions?

No. Your offer, audience, advertising and follow-up also affect results. Test the full journey.
