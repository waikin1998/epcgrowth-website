EPC MARKETING / PRACTICAL GUIDES

A campaign brief your team can actually use.

A practical campaign brief for Malaysian businesses: define your offer, budget, audience, creative assets and enquiry follow-up before hiring an agency.

A useful brief makes decisions explicit before money is spent. Use this worksheet for a Facebook Ads, Instagram Ads, Google Ads or landing page project. It is a planning tool, not a promise of performance.

Download the free brief ↓

1. Define the offer and one primary action

Write the exact service or product, eligibility, price conditions and availability. Choose one primary action: a qualified enquiry, a booking request or a purchase. Do not treat a button click as a completed sale.

2. Describe the customer and service area

Record the need you solve, the locations you genuinely serve, preferred languages and common objections. Separate known customer evidence from assumptions to test. Do not include personal customer details in a brief shared publicly.

3. Separate the budget

List media spend, management, creative production and website work separately. Identify the approval owner and a spending ceiling. Agree the first review date rather than promising an arbitrary number of leads.

4. List deliverables and permissions

Specify image sizes, video lengths, copy versions, revision rounds and deadlines. Confirm rights to logos, footage, music and testimonials. Do not send passwords: arrange appropriate account access through the relevant platform.

5. Plan what happens after an enquiry

Name the person handling replies, business hours, qualification questions and how outcomes will be recorded. Agree what makes a lead relevant before comparing campaign costs.

Example: a beauty service campaign

For a treatment enquiry campaign, a brief might specify the approved offer, actual service location, approved photography, a consultation enquiry as the next step and a staff member responsible for replies. This is an illustrative planning example, not a treatment claim or a client performance result.

Before approving the brief

  • Can the landing page fulfil the exact promise made in the ad?
  • Are exclusions and promotional conditions visible?
  • Are account access and asset ownership agreed?
  • Can the team handle the enquiries it receives?
  • Is there a defined review date and a record of changes?
