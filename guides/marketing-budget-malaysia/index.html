How to prepare a marketing budget in Malaysia.

Prepare a marketing budget for your Malaysian business. Separate ad spend, management, creative and website costs, then compare project scope and measurement.

Quick answer: separate the money paid to advertising platforms from the work required to create, manage and measure a campaign. There is no single budget that guarantees enquiries for every business. Begin with your offer, audience, capacity and an amount you can afford to test.

Cost categories · Your brief · Measurement · Questions to ask

1. Separate four cost categories

  • Media spend: the amount allocated to advertising delivery. Confirm which account is billed and how spending limits are handled.
  • Management: planning, setup, ongoing reviews and reporting. Ask which activities and platforms are included.
  • Creative production: copy, images, editing or filming. Agree quantities, formats, revision rounds and usage rights.
  • Website and measurement: landing page work, hosting, forms and conversion setup may be separate costs.

Request an itemised proposal. A smaller quote is not necessarily better value if it leaves out essential production or measurement work. Conversely, do not pay for channels or deliverables you cannot explain a business need for.

2. Write a one-page brief

Describe your product or service, locations you actually serve, typical customer questions and the next action you want a visitor to take. Add your available assets, website URL, preferred launch window and internal approval contact. State any restrictions on promotions or claims.

Distinguish the overall project budget from a recurring monthly media budget. Ask what happens after an initial test: which work continues, what can be paused, and what ownership or access is retained.

3. Choose the channel around the buying journey

Google Search advertising can address people already searching for a relevant service. Facebook and Instagram advertising can introduce a visual offer. A dedicated landing page helps explain one offer and its next step. These channels are not interchangeable, and more channels are not automatically better.

4. Define a qualified enquiry

Before reviewing a cost per lead, agree what counts as a lead. A button click, a submitted form, a qualified conversation and a completed sale are different events. Keep a simple record of enquiry source, relevance, response status and outcome while handling customer data appropriately.

For an internal planning calculation, cost per qualified enquiry equals the costs you choose to include divided by the number of qualified enquiries in the same period. Label whether that figure includes only media or also agency and production costs. If there are no qualified enquiries, report that directly instead of presenting a misleading ratio.

5. Questions to ask before agreeing a proposal

  • What is included, excluded and billed separately?
  • Who owns the advertising accounts, website and final assets?
  • Which conversions will be measured and how will tracking be checked?
  • How many creative revisions are included?
  • When will we review the work and decide what to change?
  • Who follows up with enquiries, and what happens when capacity is full?

No universal price or guaranteed outcome

This guide does not provide a market-rate benchmark or an EPC price list. Quotes depend on the scope, assets, production requirements and ongoing responsibilities. Share your project brief with EPC Marketing to discuss suitable work, or review our creative services and portfolio first.
